Types of bail

  • Regular bail after arrest, before the Magistrate (BNSS s. 480) or the Sessions Court (BNSS s. 483)
  • Anticipatory bail when arrest is apprehended in a non-bailable offence (BNSS s. 482, formerly CrPC s. 438)
  • Interim bail pending the hearing of a bail application
  • Default bail when the charge-sheet is not filed in time (BNSS s. 187(3), formerly CrPC s. 167(2))
  • Bail in special cases: NDPS (s. 37 conditions), Bihar Prohibition and Excise Act, SC/ST Act and POCSO cases

Documents usually needed

  • Copy of the FIR, or the police station and case number
  • Any earlier bail or remand order
  • Identity proof of the accused
  • Details and documents of two sureties, such as land records (khatiyan / rasid) or identity proof

Common questions

What is the difference between bail and anticipatory bail?

Regular bail is sought after arrest. Anticipatory bail is sought before arrest, by a person who fears arrest in a non-bailable offence.

Where is bail filed in Motihari?

Depending on the offence and stage, a bail application is filed before the Judicial Magistrate or Chief Judicial Magistrate, or before the Sessions Court, at the Civil Court, Motihari. Excise and NDPS cases go to the special courts.

What is default bail?

If the police do not file the charge-sheet within 60 or 90 days, depending on the offence, the accused can become entitled to bail as of right under BNSS s. 187(3).
