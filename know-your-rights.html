Know your rights

General information on the rights of a person who is arrested, and where to find free legal aid. This is not legal advice.

Rights on arrest

  • Grounds of arrest. The arrested person must be told the full particulars of the offence and the grounds for arrest, and, for a bailable offence, that they are entitled to be released on bail (BNSS s. 47; Article 22(1)).
  • Information to family. The police must inform a relative or friend named by the arrested person, and record it (BNSS s. 48).
  • 24-hour rule. No one may be kept in police custody for more than 24 hours without an order of a Magistrate, excluding travel time (BNSS s. 58; Article 22(2)).
  • Right to an advocate. The arrested person may meet an advocate of their choice during interrogation, though not throughout it (BNSS s. 38; Article 22(1)).
  • Medical examination. An arrested person is to be medically examined, and may ask for an examination to show injuries.
  • Women. Except in exceptional circumstances, and then only with the permission of a Judicial Magistrate, a woman should not be arrested after sunset or before sunrise (BNSS s. 43(5)).
  • No forced confession. No one can be compelled to be a witness against themselves (Article 20(3)), and a confession to the police is not admissible as evidence.

Arrest is not automatic

For offences punishable with imprisonment of up to seven years, the police are expected to consider whether arrest is necessary, and may instead issue a notice of appearance (BNSS s. 35(3), formerly CrPC s. 41A). This safeguard was emphasised by the Supreme Court in Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar (2014), and the Court has issued further guidelines on bail in Satender Kumar Antil v. CBI (2022).

Zero FIR and e-FIR

Information about a cognisable offence can be given at any police station, regardless of where the offence took place (a “Zero FIR”), and can also be given electronically, to be signed within three days (BNSS s. 173). You are entitled to a free copy of the FIR.

Free legal aid

Women, children, members of Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes, persons in custody, persons with disabilities, victims of trafficking or disasters, industrial workmen and persons below the income limit are entitled to free legal services under section 12 of the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987. Contact the District Legal Services Authority (DLSA), East Champaran at the Civil Court, Motihari, or the NALSA helpline.
